## Accuracy of nutrition values

Data on packaged products comes from <a href="https://world.openfoodfacts.org/" rel="noopener">Open Food Facts</a>, a collaborative database maintained by volunteers: anyone can add or edit a product. Values may be <strong>incomplete, out of date or wrong</strong>, and a product's recipe can change without the database recording it.

The generic foods already included in the app use public reference values (USDA FoodData Central) per 100 g of edible portion: these are averages, not the exact composition of what is on your plate.

Alba never invents a value: when calories are missing it converts them from kilojoules or derives them from macronutrients, and if nothing useful is available the product is not shown at all. Foods with partial data stay visible but are marked as incomplete. Even so, <strong>the most reliable figure is the label on the product in your hand</strong>: where they differ, trust the label.

The maths also depends on how precise you are: a quantity estimated by eye produces a total estimated by eye.
